# Attention → Transformer: resources

Every resource discovered for this topic, with the shortest summary that says what it's useful for. IDs are stable: concepts, maps and the design review refer to them.

> **Coverage note:** first discovery pass, 2026-11-07. Searched per subtopic (bottleneck, RNN attention, self-attention/QKV, multi-head, position/RoPE, masking, FFN, residual/norm, decoder-only, cost and limits) across papers, lectures, books, blogs, videos, interactive tools and code. Later searches mostly returned resources already listed. Not yet covered: a dedicated podcast, and a primary source for 3Blue1Brown's chapter 5 (link not retrieved).

| ID | Resource | Type | Depth | Subtopics | Summary | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio, *Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate* (2014, ICLR 2015) | paper | deep | bottleneck, RNN attention | Names the fixed-length vector as the bottleneck; decoder soft-searches all encoder states per output word. The origin of additive attention. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473) |
| R2 | Sutskever, Vinyals & Le, *Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks* (2014) | paper | deep | bottleneck | The LSTM encoder-decoder that squeezes a whole sentence into one vector; the baseline attention was built to fix. | [link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3215v3.pdf) |
| R3 | Cho et al., *On the Properties of Neural Machine Translation: Encoder–Decoder Approaches* (2014) | paper | intermediate | bottleneck | Measured evidence that encoder-decoder translation quality drops quickly as sentences get longer. The failing case, in data. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1259) |
| R4 | Brownlee, *Encoder-Decoder RNN Models for Neural Machine Translation* (Machine Learning Mastery) | blog | intro | bottleneck | Plain-language walk through the Sutskever and Cho encoder-decoder models, including the length-degradation chart. | [link](https://machinelearningmastery.com/encoder-decoder-recurrent-neural-network-models-neural-machine-translation/) |
| R5 | Luong, Pham & Manning, *Effective Approaches to Attention-based NMT* (2015) | paper | deep | RNN attention | Simpler multiplicative (dot-product) attention; global vs local attention; up to 5 BLEU gain over no attention. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04025) |
| R6 | Wu et al., *Google's Neural Machine Translation System* (GNMT, 2016) | paper | intermediate | RNN attention, residuals | Production proof: 8+8 stacked LSTMs with attention and residual connections, cutting errors ~60% vs phrase-based Google Translate. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08144) |
| R7 | Cheng, Dong & Lapata, *Long Short-Term Memory-Networks for Machine Reading* (2016) | paper | deep | self-attention origins | Early intra-attention: a reader attends back over its own earlier words instead of compressing them. Precursor to self-attention. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06733v1) |
| R8 | Vaswani et al., *Attention Is All You Need* (2017) | paper | deep | all Transformer subtopics | The Transformer: attention only, no recurrence; scaled dot-product, multi-head, sinusoidal positions, masking, residual + norm; faster training. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) |
| R9 | He et al., *Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition* (2015) | paper | intermediate | residuals | Origin of skip connections that add a layer's input to its output, making very deep networks trainable. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385v1) |
| R10 | Ba, Kiros & Hinton, *Layer Normalization* (2016) | paper | intermediate | normalization | Normalizes each example across its own features, independent of batch; the norm used inside Transformer blocks. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06450v1) |
| R11 | Xiong et al., *On Layer Normalization in the Transformer Architecture* (2020) | paper | deep | normalization, training | Why the original post-norm needs learning-rate warm-up; moving norm inside the residual branch (pre-norm) trains stably. | [link](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/xiong20b.html) |
| R12 | Su et al., *RoFormer: Rotary Position Embedding* (2021) | paper | deep | position | RoPE: encodes position by rotating queries and keys, so attention scores depend on relative distance. Used by most modern LLMs. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864v5) |
| R13 | Geva et al., *Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories* (2021) | paper | deep | feed-forward | FFN layers hold two-thirds of parameters and act like pattern-to-next-token memories; lower layers shallow, upper layers semantic. | [link](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.446) |
| R14 | Radford et al., *Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training* (GPT-1, 2018) | paper | intermediate | decoder-only | Keeps only the decoder: 12 masked self-attention blocks trained on next-token prediction. The template for GPT-style LLMs. | [link](https://cdn.openai.com/research-covers/language-unsupervised/language_understanding_paper.pdf) |
| R15 | Elhage et al. (Anthropic), *A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits* (2021) | paper / interactive | deep | residual stream, heads | Views each layer as reading from and writing to a shared residual stream; heads as independent additive units (QK = where, OV = what). | [link](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/) |
| R16 | Dao et al., *FlashAttention* (2022) | paper | deep | cost | Attention's time and memory grow quadratically with length; FlashAttention computes it exactly with far fewer memory transfers. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135) |
| R17 | Liu et al., *Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts* (2023) | paper | intermediate | limits | Models use information at the start and end of long inputs best; accuracy drops for facts placed in the middle. | [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172v3) |
| R18 | Alammar, *Visualizing a Neural Machine Translation Model (Seq2seq with Attention)* (2018) | blog | intro | bottleneck, RNN attention | Animated walkthrough of encoder-decoder RNNs, then attention scoring each encoder state per output word. Best visual first pass. | [link](https://jalammar.github.io/visualizing-neural-machine-translation-mechanics-of-seq2seq-models-with-attention/) |
| R19 | Alammar, *The Illustrated Transformer* (2018) | blog | intro | all Transformer subtopics | The classic picture-by-picture explainer of Q/K/V, multi-head, positional encoding and encoder/decoder stacks. | [link](https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/) |
| R20 | Rush (Harvard NLP), *The Annotated Transformer* (2018, updated 2022) | notebook | deep | all Transformer subtopics | The paper interleaved with runnable PyTorch, line by line. Use to verify any mechanic in code. | [link](https://nlp.seas.harvard.edu/annotated-transformer) |
| R21 | Olah & Carter, *Attention and Augmented Recurrent Neural Networks* (Distill, 2016) | interactive article | intro | RNN attention | Interactive diagrams of attention on RNNs: focus everywhere, to different degrees, so it stays trainable. | [link](https://distill.pub/2016/augmented-rnns/) |
| R22 | Weng, *Attention? Attention!* (2018) and *The Transformer Family v2* (2023), Lil'Log | blog | intermediate | RNN attention, variants | Survey of attention scoring functions (additive, dot, scaled) and later Transformer variants, with notation side by side. | [link](https://lilianweng.github.io/tags/attention) |
| R23 | 3Blue1Brown, *Attention in transformers, step-by-step* (Deep Learning ch. 6, 2024) | video | intro | self-attention, multi-head, masking | 26-minute animated explainer of Q/K/V, softmax weights, masking and multi-head, with GPT-3 parameter counts. | [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMlx5fFNoYc) |
| R24 | Karpathy, *Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out* (2023) | video | intermediate | self-attention, masking, block | Two-hour build from averaging past tokens to masked self-attention, multi-head, FFN, residuals and norm. | [link](https://classcentral.com/classroom/youtube-let-s-build-gpt-from-scratch-in-code-spelled-out-127034) |
